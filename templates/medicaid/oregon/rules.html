A new choice for states (hold harmless through June 30, 2027)

Governor Tina Kotek directed OHA to use the federal hold harmless option through June 30, 2027 (subject to federal approval). You will not be turned down for OHP or lose coverage through June 30, 2027 solely for not meeting the work or activity rules—though checking still starts Jan. 1, 2027 (including Healthier Oregon adults ages 19 to 64) and other rules like income still apply. {{ src('MED-S01') }} {{ src('MED-S07') }} {{ src('MED-S53') }} {{ src('MED-S41') }}

Meet the rules any one way

{{ m.icon('check') }}Way 1

1. Household income of $580 or more a month

Counts the combined income of your Medicaid household ($7.25 federal minimum wage × 80 hours). Two adults in the household do not each need $580. {{ src('MED-S27') }}

{{ m.icon('school-cap') }}Way 2

2. School at least half-time

Your school decides what counts as half-time—including college, career or trade school, high school, and GED programs. {{ src('MED-S01') }}

{{ m.icon('hours-meter') }}Way 3

3. 80 hours a month (~20 hours a week)

Combine paid work, self-employment, work for housing/goods, internships, school, job training, or tracked nonprofit/public volunteering. {{ src('MED-S01') }}

Good to know

  • Each adult checked individually for hours: One person's hours or school do not cover another adult, though the $580 income test uses combined household income. {{ src('MED-S05') }} {{ src('MED-S27') }}
  • No monthly reports: OHP checks electronic records first when you apply and again at renewal—not every month. {{ src('MED-S04') }}
  • Exemptions: People with a disability, pregnancy, caregiving duties, or Medicare do not have to meet these rules. See every exemption or take the 1-minute check. {{ src('MED-S02') }} {{ src('MED-S03') }}
