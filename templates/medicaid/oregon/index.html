Applying for OHP? New rules start Jan. 1, 2027.

Why this site exists: The state explains the new OHP rules across more than a dozen documents. This site puts what applicants need in one place, in plain words. Each page lists its official sources.

{{ m.icon('info') }}Policy update

Update, Nov. 2026: Oregon delays work-rule enforcement

Oregon's choice: Governor Tina Kotek directed OHA to use the federal hold harmless choice through June 30, 2027 (subject to federal approval). OHA says you will not be turned down for OHP or lose coverage through June 30, 2027 if you do not meet the work or activity rules. {{ src('MED-S07') }} {{ src('MED-S53') }} {{ src('MED-S41') }}

Still starting: The work or activity rules still start Jan. 1, 2027. The checking starts then too.

Federal choice: On Nov. 13, 2026, the federal government gave states this choice. Through June 30, 2027, a state may choose not to turn anyone down, and not to end anyone's coverage, only for not meeting the work or activity rules. The state must still check whether each person meets the rules or does not have to. {{ src('MED-S41') }}

What it does not do: This choice does not protect anyone from being turned down or losing coverage for other reasons, such as income.

Court case: On Nov. 5, 2026, Oregon, Washington, and two other states sued the federal government over the work or activity rules (State of Oregon v. Oz, No. 6:26-cv-02435). Oral argument is set for Jan. 12, 2027. As of Nov. 21, 2026, no court order has changed these rules. {{ src('MED-S44') }} {{ src('MED-S45') }} {{ src('MED-S46') }}

Have medical bills? Read this before Jan. 1, 2027.

Through Dec. 31, 2026, OHP can help with medical bills from up to 3 months before you apply. {{ src('MED-S01') }}

Starting Jan. 1, 2027, OHP can help with medical bills from 1 month before you apply for most adults ages 19 to 64. {{ src('MED-S01') }}

The short version

  • What's new: Starting Jan. 1, 2027, many adults ages 19 to 64 must meet new work or activity rules to get OHP. {{ src('MED-S01') }}
  • Good news: Many people already meet them, or don't have to.
  • When OHP looks: The month before you apply. {{ src('MED-S04') }}
  • Records first: OHP checks its own records, like payroll and school records, before it asks you for anything.

Meet the rules any one way

  • {{ m.icon('income') }}Income Your household brings in $580 or more a month. {{ src('MED-S27') }}
    or
  • {{ m.icon('school') }}School You go to school at least half-time. {{ src('MED-S01') }}
    or
  • {{ m.icon('clock') }}80 hours You spend 80 hours a month on work, volunteering, school, or job training. Add them up. {{ src('MED-S01') }}

What counts, in plain words

You may not have to if you

  • Are pregnant, or were in the last 12 months {{ src('MED-S03') }}
  • Care for a child 13 or younger, or for someone with a disability {{ src('MED-S03') }}
  • Have a serious or disabling health condition {{ src('MED-S03') }}
  • Are American Indian or Alaska Native
  • Were in foster care at 18 and are under 26

There are more reasons. OHP checks some reasons itself. For others, you just tell OHP. See every reason

What to do

  1. Take the 1-minute check. Your answers stay on your device.
  2. Apply online, by phone, in person, or on paper.
  3. Answer every question about work, school, and volunteering. If a reason fits you, tell OHP.

{{ m.icon('info') }}Bottom line

Apply if you think you qualify

If you think you qualify, apply. If OHP says no, you can apply again any time.
