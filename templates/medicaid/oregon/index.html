Update, Nov. 2026: Oregon delays work-rule enforcement

Oregon's choice: Governor Tina Kotek directed OHA to use the federal hold harmless choice through June 30, 2027 (subject to federal approval). OHA says you will not be turned down for OHP or lose coverage through June 30, 2027 solely for not meeting the work or activity rules, though checking still starts Jan. 1, 2027 and other rules like income still apply. {{ src('MED-S07') }} {{ src('MED-S53') }} {{ src('MED-S41') }}

{% call m.deeper('Federal hold harmless details & Oregon v. Oz lawsuit', 'or-update-details') %}

Federal choice: On Nov. 13, 2026, the federal government gave states this choice through June 30, 2027 while still checking whether each person meets the rules or has an exemption. {{ src('MED-S41') }}

Court case: On Nov. 5, 2026, Oregon, Washington, and two other states sued the federal government over the work or activity rules (State of Oregon v. Oz, No. 6:26-cv-02435; oral argument set for Jan. 12, 2027). As of Nov. 21, 2026, no court order has changed these rules. {{ src('MED-S44') }} {{ src('MED-S45') }} {{ src('MED-S46') }}

More about this choice

{{ m.icon('alert') }}Deadline

Have medical bills? Read this before Jan. 1, 2027.

Through Dec. 31, 2026, OHP can help with medical bills from up to 3 months before you apply. {{ src('MED-S01') }}

Starting Jan. 1, 2027, OHP can help with medical bills from 1 month before you apply for most adults ages 19 to 64. More about past medical bills {{ src('MED-S01') }}

Meet the rules any one way

Starting Jan. 1, 2027, OHP checks its own payroll and school records first for the month before you apply. {{ src('MED-S01') }} {{ src('MED-S04') }}

  • {{ m.icon('income') }}Income Your household brings in $580 or more a month. {{ src('MED-S27') }}
    or
  • {{ m.icon('school') }}School You go to school at least half-time. {{ src('MED-S01') }}
    or
  • {{ m.icon('clock') }}80 hours You spend 80 hours a month on work, volunteering, school, or job training. Add them up. {{ src('MED-S01') }}

{{ m.icon('check') }} Any 1 way meets the rules. What counts, in plain words

You may not have to if you

  • Are pregnant, or were in the last 12 months {{ src('MED-S03') }}
  • Care for a child 13 or younger, or for someone with a disability {{ src('MED-S03') }}
  • Have a serious or disabling health condition {{ src('MED-S03') }}
  • Are American Indian or Alaska Native, or were in foster care at 18 and are under 26 {{ src('MED-S02') }}

There are more exemptions. See every reason

What to do

  1. Take the 1-minute check. Your answers stay on your device.
  2. Apply online, by phone, in person, or on paper. If OHP says no, you can apply again any time. {{ src('MED-S06') }}
  3. Answer every question about work, school, and volunteering. If a reason fits you, tell OHP. {{ src('MED-S05') }} {{ src('MED-S08') }}
