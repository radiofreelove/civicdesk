Update, Nov. 17, 2026: Washington delays work-rule enforcement

Washington's announcement: On Nov. 17, 2026, HCA announced Washington will pursue delayed enforcement of the work rules (pending federal approval). Through June 30, 2027, people in Apple Health for Adults will not lose or be denied coverage for not meeting the work rules. Six-month renewals and the 1-month past-bills limit still start Jan. 1, 2027. {{ src('MED-WA27') }} {{ src('MED-WA22') }}

Federal choice: The work rules and checking still start Jan. 1, 2027, and HCA says clients will still be asked to verify. Through June 30, 2027, HCA says no one will lose or be denied Apple Health for not meeting them, pending federal approval of Washington's plan. On Nov. 13, 2026, the federal government gave states this hold harmless choice through June 30, 2027, subject to federal approval. {{ src('MED-S41') }} {{ src('MED-WA27') }}

What it does not do: This choice does not protect anyone from being turned down or losing coverage for other reasons, such as income.

Court case: On Nov. 5, 2026, Washington, Oregon, and two other states sued the federal government over the work rules (State of Oregon v. Oz, No. 6:26-cv-02435). Oral argument is set for Jan. 12, 2027. As of Nov. 21, 2026, no court order has changed these rules. {{ src('MED-WA14') }} {{ src('MED-S44') }} {{ src('MED-S45') }} {{ src('MED-S46') }}

Have medical bills? Read this before Jan. 1, 2027.

The short version

  • The name: Apple Health is Washington's name for Medicaid.
  • What's new: New federal work rules for Apple Health for Adults start Jan. 1, 2027, though HCA announced on Nov. 17, 2026 that it will delay work-rule coverage loss through June 30, 2027. {{ src('MED-WA01') }} {{ src('MED-WA27') }}
  • Right now: The Health Care Authority (HCA) tells members to keep their contact information up to date and carefully read any notices or text messages from HCA. {{ src('MED-WA01') }} {{ src('MED-WA27') }}
  • Who: Only people in Apple Health for Adults. That means ages 19 to 64, no Medicare, living in Washington, with low income. {{ src('MED-WA15') }}
  • Records first: Washington checks its own data first. HCA estimated that it could check about 64 percent of people in Apple Health for Adults this way, or find they are exempt.
  • Shorter coverage: Starting Jan. 1, 2027, Apple Health for Adults moves from 12-month to 6-month renewals (American Indian and Alaska Native adults keep 12-month renewals). {{ src('MED-WA16') }}
  • Notices: HCA sends notices by mail or email. Keep your contact information up to date.

The rules, in plain words

You may not have to if you

  • Are pregnant, or in the 12 months after a pregnancy {{ src('MED-WA05') }}
  • Are a parent, caretaker, or guardian of a child under 14 {{ src('MED-WA22') }}
  • Are medically frail {{ src('MED-WA05') }}
  • Are American Indian or Alaska Native
  • Are a foster youth, or a former foster youth under 26

Many people do not have to meet the rule at all. HCA's fact sheet calls these reasons exemptions. See every reason

What to do

  1. Take the 1-minute check. Your answers stay on your device.
  2. Apply online, by phone, on paper, or in person. Apple Health enrollment is open all year.
  3. You may tell the state your own income, work, or exemption status (self-attestation, including for medical frailty through 2027). If state records cannot confirm your eligibility, a letter may ask for proof within 15 days. {{ src('MED-WA04') }} {{ src('MED-WA22') }}

{{ m.icon('info') }}Bottom line

Only Washington decides

This guide says "you may." Only Washington decides who meets the rules or is exempt.
