A new choice for states

On Nov. 13, 2026, the federal government gave states a choice through June 30, 2027 not to turn anyone down or end coverage solely for not meeting work or activity rules (a hold harmless period). As of Nov. 21, 2026, Utah has not announced a choice, and its pages do not say what Utah will choose. {{ src('MED-S41') }} {{ src('MED-U01') }} {{ src('MED-U02') }}

Other Utah Medicaid programs outside Adult Expansion and TAM do not have these rules. {{ src('MED-U01') }} {{ src('MED-U04') }} {{ src('MED-U13') }}

Meet the rules any one way

{{ m.icon('check') }}Way 1

1. Income of at least $580 a month

Utah's pages say "earning at least $580 a month" ($7.25 × 80 hours, or about $134 a week; seasonal workers can average over 6 months). The federal rule counts combined Medicaid household income while checking each adult's hours separately—ask DWS how it counts yours. {{ src('MED-U01') }} {{ src('MED-S27') }}

{{ m.icon('school-cap') }}Way 2

2. School at least half-time

You attend school on a schedule your school calls half-time or more. {{ src('MED-U01') }}

{{ m.icon('hours-meter') }}Way 3

3. 80 hours a month (~20 hours a week)

Combine work, job training, school, or unpaid volunteering with an organized community program. Not sure if an activity counts? Ask DWS or a free helper. {{ src('MED-U01') }}

Proof rules in Utah

DWS checks work, school, and benefit databases before asking you for anything. If records do not show your activity, Utah Code 26B-3-142.1 requires documentary proof rather than self-attestation alone. See every exemption or take the 1-minute check. {{ src('MED-U04') }} {{ src('MED-U39') }}
