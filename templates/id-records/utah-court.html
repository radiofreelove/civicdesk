Can you ask for a name change?

  • You must have lived in the county where you file for at least one year. {{ src('ID-UT-CODE-42-1') }}
  • You cannot file if you are in the custody of the Utah Department of Corrections. You also cannot file if Adult Probation and Parole supervises your probation or parole. {{ src('ID-UT-COURTS-SEX-CHANGE') }}
  • You must say on the petition whether you are on the sex, kidnap, and child abuse offender registry. {{ src('ID-UT-CODE-42-1') }}
  • The court says you cannot change your name to avoid debts or criminal penalties, or to commit fraud. The new name also cannot be "bizarre, unduly lengthy, ridiculous, or offensive to common decency." {{ src('ID-UT-COURTS-SEX-CHANGE') }}

What you must prove for a sex designation change

The judge must find "clear and convincing evidence" of all six of these. That means strong proof, more than "more likely than not." {{ src('ID-UT-CODE-26B-8-111') }}

  1. You are not involved in any kind of lawsuit.
  2. You are not under Department of Corrections custody or supervision (the law's word is "offender").
  3. You are not asking in order to commit a crime, avoid debts, interfere with other people's rights, affect a criminal sentence, or commit fraud.
  4. You have transitioned to the sex you are asking for.
  5. You have lived as that sex, in a consistent way, for at least six months.
  6. The sex now on your birth certificate causes you "clinically significant distress or impairment."

The judge also weighs two kinds of evidence: {{ src('ID-UT-CODE-26B-8-111') }}

  • your medical history, care, or treatment related to transition
  • proof that the sex you are asking for is sincerely held and part of your core identity
{% call m.box('do', 'Proof the court suggests gathering') %}
  • A letter signed by a licensed medical provider saying the current sex on your birth certificate causes you clinically significant distress.
  • Proof that you have lived as your sex for the past six months.
  • Proof that this change is a true and important part of your identity.

Who can see your case

When you ask for a sex designation change, including a name and sex change together, the court marks the case private. The papers you file are not public. The case history, public hearings, and records of public hearings still are. {{ src('ID-UT-COURTS-SEX-CHANGE') }} {{ ext('ID-UT-COURTS-NONPUBLIC') }}.

The courts page says this only about sex change requests. We read that to mean a name-only case is public. {{ m.badge('our_reading') }} {{ src('ID-UT-COURTS-SEX-CHANGE') }}

Changes for someone under 18

For a child's name change, file in the district court in the county where the child has lived for the past year. If the other parent agrees, they sign a consent form. If not, they must be formally served with the papers. There is a hearing. {{ src('ID-UT-COURTS-MINOR-CHANGE') }}

Utah has strict rules for a child's sex designation change. {{ src('ID-UT-CODE-26B-8-111') }}

  • A judge cannot grant it for a child younger than 15 years and 6 months.
  • A judge cannot grant it unless the parent or guardian with legal custody gives permission.
  • For a child 15 years and 6 months or older who is not emancipated, the court appoints a guardian ad litem, a lawyer for the child. Unless the court finds them unable to pay, the parent or guardian pays for it.
  • Known parents get notice and can object. The hearing is closed to the public.
  • The judge must find clear and convincing evidence that the change is in the child's best interest. The judge must also find it would not create a risk of harm.
  • The order does not take effect until the child is at least 16.

Good questions

{% call m.qa("What does \"involved in any kind of lawsuit\" mean for me?", 'q-lawsuit') %}

The law uses those words and does not define them further on the page we read. If you are part of any court case, such as a divorce, custody case, debt case, or eviction, talk to a lawyer before you file. {{ src('ID-UT-CODE-26B-8-111') }} {{ m.badge('our_reading') }}

{% endcall %} {% call m.qa("Can I ask the court to use my pronouns?", 'q-pronouns') %}

{% endcall %} {% call m.qa("What if the judge says no?", 'q-denied') %}

You can appeal. Utah Courts says appeals can be complicated and suggests getting legal advice. {{ ext('ID-UT-COURTS-LEGAL-HELP') }}. {{ src('ID-UT-COURTS-SEX-CHANGE') }}

{% endcall %} {% call m.qa("Could I file in another state instead?", 'q-other-state') %}

Name changes are usually filed where you live. Oregon's law lets any Oregon circuit court hear a legal sex change. But we have not found an official answer for people who live outside Oregon. A Utah office also might not accept another state's order. Talk to a lawyer first. {{ m.badge('our_reading') }} Read the Oregon question.
