Public or sealed name change

You pick one of two courts. {{ src('ID-WA-RCW-4-24-130') }}

Washington's two ways to change a name
OptionPublic name changeSealed name change
CourtAny district court in WashingtonAny superior court in Washington
Who can use itAnyonePeople whose name change relates to gender expression or identity. Also people with certain safety or immigration reasons.
Who can see itThe publicNo public access to the filing, the hearing, or the order. The file opens only for you, your guardian or representative, or by court order for good cause.
Not available toSome people under Department of Corrections supervision, and people required to register as a sex offender, face extra stepsPeople under Department of Corrections jurisdiction, or people required to register as a sex offender

A court order that confirms your gender

This is optional. You might want one if another state's birth certificate office or another agency asks for a court order. Washington does not need it for a Washington license or birth certificate. {{ ext('ID-WA-WLH-GENDER-ORDER') }}. {{ src('ID-WA-WLH-GENDER-ORDER') }}

Changes for someone under 18

  • Name: a parent or guardian can ask for a child's name change. The law allows a sealed superior court case when the reason relates to the child's gender expression or identity. {{ src('ID-WA-RCW-4-24-130') }}
  • Parents must get notice. Some courts also ask for the child's consent at age 13 or older. {{ src('ID-WA-WLH-NAME') }}
  • Birth certificate: use {{ ext('ID-WA-DOH-422-144') }}. A parent or guardian and a licensed provider sign it. See Washington birth certificate steps. {{ src('ID-WA-DOH-SEX') }}

Good questions

{% call m.qa("Do I have to file in the county where I live?", 'q-county') %}

No. The law lets you file a public name change in any district court in the state. A sealed name change can go to any superior court in the state. {{ src('ID-WA-RCW-4-24-130') }}

{% endcall %} {% call m.qa("Who can see a sealed name change?", 'q-sealed') %}

No one in the public. The file opens only if a judge orders it for good cause, or if you, your guardian, or your representative ask. {{ src('ID-WA-RCW-4-24-130') }}
