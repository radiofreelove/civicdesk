Passports

  • On January 20, 2025, an executive order changed the rules. It said passports, visas, and Global Entry cards must show the holder's sex as the order defines it. {{ src('ID-US-EO-14168') }}
  • New and renewed passports now show the sex assigned at birth, no matter your name change or other documents. The X marker, offered since 2022, was eliminated. {{ src('ID-US-HRC-PASSPORT') }}
  • The ACLU says anyone who applies for a passport is at risk of getting one with the sex assigned at birth. That includes new, corrected, replacement, and renewed passports. {{ src('ID-US-ACLU-ORR') }}
  • The State Department says existing passports stay valid for travel until they expire, you replace them, or the department invalidates them under federal regulations. {{ src('ID-US-STATE-SEX-MARKER') }}

Before you apply for a passport

{% call m.box('watch', 'Watch out') %}

If your current passport shows the marker you want, be careful. Any new application, even for a name change, may come back with the sex assigned at birth. {{ src('ID-US-HRC-PASSPORT') }} {{ src('ID-US-ACLU-ORR') }} Think about whether you need a new passport now, and talk to a lawyer if you are unsure. {{ m.badge('our_reading') }}

{% endcall %}

You can still change your name on a passport with a certified court order. But the Human Rights Campaign warns of a risk. Asking for a name change could lead the State Department to change your passport's sex marker to match your birth sex. That can happen even if you had a different marker before. {{ src('ID-US-HRC-PASSPORT') }}

The State Department explains which form to use for a name change. {{ ext('ID-US-STATE-CHANGE') }}.

If the State Department asks for early records

The Human Rights Campaign reports that some applicants got letters asking for "early public records" showing their name and sex at birth. Its advice: do not respond until you talk to an LGBTQ+-affirming attorney or legal organization. Until then, do not send court orders, medical records, or other documents about your transition. {{ src('ID-US-HRC-PASSPORT') }}

Free help: the {{ ext('ID-US-LAMBDA-HELPDESK') }}.

The court case

  • The ACLU sued over the passport policy in a case called Orr v. Trump. {{ src('ID-US-ACLU-ORR') }}
  • A federal court blocked the policy in June 2025. In November 2025, the U.S. Supreme Court let the government enforce it during the appeal. {{ src('ID-US-HRC-PASSPORT') }} {{ src('ID-US-ORR-1ST-CIR') }}
  • On June 5, 2026, the First Circuit dismissed the appeal as moot and vacated the preliminary injunctions. The plaintiffs had dropped their request for a preliminary injunction so the main case could resume in trial court. No preliminary injunction is in effect. {{ src('ID-US-ORR-1ST-CIR') }}
  • As of November 9, 2026, Orr v. Trump continues in trial-court "discovery" (where each side gathers information) through at least spring 2027. {{ src('ID-US-ACLU-ORR') }}

With no court injunction in effect, the State Department's birth-sex rule stays in place for new and renewed passports. Existing passports stay valid for travel until they expire, you replace them, or the department invalidates them under federal regulations. {{ src('ID-US-ORR-1ST-CIR') }} {{ src('ID-US-ACLU-ORR') }} {{ src('ID-US-STATE-SEX-MARKER') }}

Rules can change if the courts rule. We will update this page, but check the {{ ext('ID-US-ACLU-ORR') }} for the latest.

Social Security

  • Name: after you legally change your name, update it by asking for a replacement Social Security card. You may be able to start online. {{ ext('ID-US-SSA-NAME') }}. {{ src('ID-US-SSA-NAME') }}
  • Sex marker: Oregon's DMV reports that as of January 31, 2025, Social Security was directed to stop processing gender marker updates. The Human Rights Campaign reports the same. {{ src('ID-OR-DMV-GENDER') }} {{ src('ID-US-HRC-PASSPORT') }}
  • Why update your name first: some DMVs check your name against Social Security before they issue a card. Oregon's does. {{ src('ID-OR-DMV-GENDER') }}
{% call m.box('watch', 'June 2026 Social Security policy rules') %}

Social Security's manual (POMS RM 10212.200, revised June 5, 2026) says SSA only changes or corrects the sex field on your record to show sex at birth. To correct the sex field, SSA accepts an amended, corrected, or delayed birth certificate only with an amendment affidavit or other proof showing why it was amended, and even then only to record sex at birth. {{ src('ID-US-SSA-POMS-10212-200') }}

Legal name changes with SSA still work normally. Social Security cards do not show a sex marker. {{ src('ID-US-SSA-NAME') }} {{ src('ID-US-SSA-POMS-10212-200') }}

What still works

  • Oregon and Washington licenses still offer M, F, or X. Oregon's DMV says it has had no federal direction about markers on REAL ID cards and keeps offering all three. {{ src('ID-OR-DMV-GENDER') }} {{ src('ID-WA-DOL-GENDER') }}
  • Oregon and Washington birth certificates still offer M, F, or X through a form. Oregon, Washington.
  • Court orders: courts in all three states still change names. Oregon and Utah courts still order sex changes. Washington changes the marker with a form. Compare the states.
