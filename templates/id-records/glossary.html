Plain definitions

Words to know

{{ m.lede("Court forms use words most people never need until now. Here is what they mean, in the order you are likely to see them.") }}
Petition
The form that asks a court for something, like a new name. The person who files it is the petitioner.
Fee waiver and fee deferral
A waiver means you do not pay a court fee because you cannot afford it. A deferral, offered in Oregon, means you pay it later.
Circuit court
Oregon's main trial court. Every county has one.
District court
In Washington, a local court where you can file a public name change. In Utah, the main trial court, where name and sex changes are filed.
Superior court
Washington's main trial court. It hears sealed name changes.
Hearing
A time when you go before a judge, in person or sometimes by video, and the judge may ask you questions.
Notice
Telling someone with a legal interest, like a child's other parent, that a case was filed.
Judgment or order
The judge's signed decision. It is your proof of the change.
Notice of Entry of Judgment
In Oregon, a notice the court sends when your judgment is entered in its records. The change is official at that point. {{ src('ID-OR-OJD-ADULT-PACKET') }}
Certified copy
A copy of a court order or record with an official stamp or seal that shows it is a true copy. Most offices want this, not a photocopy.
Confidential, sealed, and private
Each state uses its own word. In Oregon, a confidential case can be seen only by you, your lawyer, the court, and people a judge allows. In Washington, a sealed name change file has no public access. In Utah, a private case keeps your filed papers from the public, but the case history and hearings are public. {{ src('ID-OR-2026-CH52') }} {{ src('ID-WA-RCW-4-24-130') }} {{ src('ID-UT-COURTS-SEX-CHANGE') }}
Address Confidentiality Program
An Oregon state program that keeps participants' addresses private. Certified participants can ask the court to seal a name change case. {{ src('ID-OR-ORS-33') }}
Gender marker (legal sex, sex designation, sex marker)
The M, F, or X on an ID or a record. This guide uses gender marker as the everyday term. Courts and agencies use their own official words. Oregon courts say legal sex (with nonbinary on the form), while Oregon DMV and Vital Records print X. Washington Department of Licensing says gender designation. Washington Department of Health and Utah courts and Vital Records say sex designation. Federal agencies say sex marker or sex.
X (nonbinary)
A marker for people who are not exclusively male or female. Oregon court forms say "nonbinary." Oregon's DMV says X stands for "not specified," and Oregon birth records list X as nonbinary. Washington describes X as a gender that is not exclusively male or female. {{ src('ID-OR-DMV-GENDER') }} {{ src('ID-OR-OHA-GENDER') }} {{ src('ID-WA-DOH-SEX') }}
Attest
To state under oath that something is true. In Oregon, you attest that you have had treatment appropriate for you to affirm your gender identity.
Clear and convincing evidence
A high level of proof: more than "more likely than not," but less than "beyond a reasonable doubt." Utah requires it for a sex designation change.
Notary and notarized
A notary is an official who checks your ID and watches you sign. Their stamp shows you signed. Banks and shipping stores often have one.
Administrative process
Changing a record by filling out an agency's form instead of going to court. Example: Oregon's birth certificate form OHA 2673.
Amendment and amendment history
An amendment is a change to an official record. In Utah, an amendment history is a separate document that lists which parts of a birth certificate changed and when.
Vital records
The state office that keeps birth certificates. Only the office in the state where you were born can change yours.
Emancipated
A person under 18 whom a court has declared legally independent from their parents.
Guardian ad litem
A person the court appoints to look out for a child's interests in a case. In Utah, the court appoints one in sex designation cases for children 15 years and 6 months or older. {{ src('ID-UT-CODE-26B-8-111') }}
Self-help center and courthouse facilitator
Court programs that help people without lawyers with forms and steps. They cannot give legal advice.
REAL ID
A license or ID card that meets federal security standards. Oregon offers M, F, or X on both REAL ID and standard cards.
